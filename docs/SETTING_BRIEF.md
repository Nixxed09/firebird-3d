# Firebird: setting brief (the Great Fires)

Decided 2026-11-11 by Phoenix (the setting) and Travis (the Rebuilders, real guns,
the shooting feel). This sits beside `docs/STORY_BIBLE.md`. Where the two disagree,
this file wins and the story bible needs updating (see "What changes" at the end).

Status: a brief. Nothing here is built. Rule R1 in `REBOOT_PLAN.md` still holds:
the look room passes the look gate before any new gameplay is written.

## 1. The idea in four lines

- The time is about 1870 to 1910, in the Old Earth realm (`GamesOS/theme-packs/tartaria-unveiled/WORLD_BIBLE.md`).
- City after city burns. The old stone cities, with their arches, domes and bell towers, are being destroyed on purpose.
- You are the Firebird. You arrive in each city during its fire or just after it.
- You fight the crews who set the fires and strip the buildings, and you save what can be saved.

## 2. Who the bad guys are

Two levels. Keep them apart in every level and every line of text.

| Group | Who they are | Where you meet them |
|---|---|---|
| **The Overseers** | The top. They decide which city is counted, burned and buried. The Reckoner speaks for them. | Rarely. A voice, a ledger, a boss at the end of an episode. |
| **The Rebuilders** | The order under the Overseers. Their public job is "rebuilding the realm after the fall". Their real job is to burn the old cities, strip the working parts out of the buildings and put plain new ones on top. | Every level. They are the enemies you fight. |

The Rebuilders are a made-up order. Do not name or copy any real group, lodge,
church or company. Give them their own badge, uniform and words.

Rebuilder crews (first list, to be designed):

| Crew | Job | How they fight |
|---|---|---|
| Torchmen | Set the fires | Close range, throw fire, run between buildings |
| Strippers | Pull the bells, coils and metal out of buildings | Carry loads, fight from cover, guard wagons |
| Wardens | Keep townsfolk out with rifles | Hold streets and rooftops at long range |
| Foremen | Run a crew | Tougher, call other crews in |
| Looters | Not Rebuilders. Townsfolk and drifters taking what is left. | Scatter and run. Often not a fight at all. |

How the Hollows fit: an open question for Phoenix and Travis (section 7).

## 3. The cities

All of these fires are real events. Recorded history gives ordinary causes (wooden
buildings, drought, wind, an earthquake). In our realm's story they were set on
purpose. That is the game's fiction, and the game should not claim it as fact.

| Episode | City | Year | What the level can be |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ashgate** (our own city, already written) | - | The star-fort city. Teaches the game. Stays as it is in the story bible. |
| 2 | Chicago | 1871 | The river, the bridges, the Water Tower still standing in a burned district |
| 3 | Boston | 1872 | Narrow granite warehouse streets, fire jumping roof to roof |
| 4 | Seattle | 1889 | The waterfront and the streets later buried a floor deep: a ready-made "buried city" |
| 5 | Jacksonville | 1901 | Heat, pine and river docks |
| 6 | Baltimore | 1904 | The harbour and the business district at night |
| 7 | Toronto | 1904 | Winter fire: snow, ice and flame together |
| 8 | San Francisco | 1906 | Earthquake, then fire, on steep hills |

Other fires to place on the map later: Hamburg 1842, St. John's 1892, Ålesund 1904.

Each city gets a "during" level (fire spreading, streets changing as buildings
fall) and an "after" level (ash, shells of buildings, Rebuilders stripping them).

Real places need care: no real person is a villain, and real people who died in
these fires are never used as set dressing.

## 4. How it should look

- Real stone, brick, iron, timber and glass from Fab packs (rule R5).
- Fire is the main light: orange against night blue. The look room's warm and cool lights already point this way.
- Old-world buildings: arches, domes, star-fort walls, bell towers. The church ruin kit is the first of these.
- Period things in the street: gas lamps, wagons, crates, fire-brigade pumps, telegraph poles.

Packs to look for on Fab (none are in the project yet):

1. 1880s to 1900s city street buildings and props.
2. Fire, smoke and ember effects.
3. Period weapons (section 5).
4. Burned and collapsed building pieces.

## 5. Weapons

**Real period guns are allowed** (Travis, 2026-11-11). Rule R7 has been changed
to say so. The rest of R7 stays: no blood, no gore, no demons or occult symbols.

| Slot | Kind | Period examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sidearm | Revolver | Single-action and double-action revolvers |
| Rifle | Lever-action or bolt-action | The main long gun |
| Close | Pump or double shotgun | For streets and doorways |
| Thrown | Fire-brigade and Rebuilder gear | Something that puts fire out or sets it |
| The Firebird's own | Ember Fist, Spark Caster | Kept. This is what makes the hero a firebird and not a soldier. |

Hits on enemies still show light and sparks, not blood.

## 6. How it should feel to play

Target (Travis, 2026-11-11): close to a grounded 2005-era war shooter that he
liked the feel of. We copy the feel and the genre's common moves, not that game's
levels, weapons, sounds, names or look.

What that means:

1. **Aim down the sights.** Holding aim brings the gun up, slows you and tightens the shot.
2. **Lean** left and right around corners.
3. **Weight.** You walk, crouch and go prone. Jumps are short. Guns kick and take time to reload.
4. **Two guns carried**, picked up from the level.
5. **Cover matters.** Streets are read by sightlines.
6. **Health comes back** if you stay out of fire for a few seconds.

This is a change from today's kit (Ember Dash, slide, release-heal, fast arena
movement). It needs a decision (section 7) and a new step in `REBOOT_PLAN.md`
after the look gate. It is not started.
