# FIREBIRD 3D

A classic 90s-style first-person shooter in the spirit of the original DOOM,
built as a NIX GAMES project. Chunky pixels, sprite demons, keycards, secret
walls, exploding barrels, and a status bar face that gets beat up as you do.

**Episode One: Knee-Deep in the Ashes** — 4 levels:

1. `E1M1: ASH GATES`
2. `E1M2: THE FURNACE`
3. `E1M3: DEMON THRONE` (boss: the Ember Knight)
4. `E1M4: RILEY'S ARENA` (final boss: Riley)

## How To Play

Just open `index.html` in a browser (double-click it). No install, no build,
no internet needed. Everything — textures, monsters, sounds, music — is
generated by code when the page loads.

Pick **NEW GAME** on the title menu, choose a difficulty, and click to begin.
Every level opens with a card showing its name and your goal.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
|-----|--------|
| `W A S D` / arrow keys | Move and strafe (left/right arrows turn) |
| Mouse | Turn and aim |
| Left click / `Ctrl` | Fire |
| `E` or `Space` | Use: open doors, press switches |
| `Shift` | Run |
| `1` `2` `3` / mouse wheel | Fist / Pistol / Shotgun |
| `Q` | Swap back to your last weapon |
| `Tab` | Automap |
| `M` | Music on/off |
| `Esc` | Pause menu |

## Menus

- **Title:** Continue (from the furthest level you've reached), New Game,
  Level Select, Options, Controls. Arrow keys or mouse, `Enter` or click.
- **Difficulty:** ROOKIE (half damage, double ammo), WARRIOR, INFERNO (1.5x damage).
- **Options:** mouse speed, volume, music, crosshair, tips, difficulty.
  Saved in the browser.
- **Pause (`Esc`):** resume, restart level, options, controls, quit. It also
  shows your goal and your kills/items/secrets/time.

## Reading the HUD

- **Crosshair** turns red over a demon and orange over a barrel. Small ticks
  flash on a hit, red ticks on a kill, grey ticks when Riley's shield blocks.
- **Red wedges** around the crosshair point at whatever just hurt you.
- **Use prompt:** face a door or switch to see `[E] OPEN`, `[E] EXIT LEVEL`,
  or `BLUE KEYCARD NEEDED`. Secret walls never show a prompt.
- **Status bar:** numbers turn orange when low and flash when critical. The
  red edge pulse means you're under 25% health. **ARMS** shows the weapons you
  own (yellow = in hand, dim = no ammo). A faint key outline means this level
  has a key you haven't found yet.
- **Automap (`Tab`):** your goal, stats, a legend, and colour-coded doors.
- **Goal marker:** once you've *seen* your next goal (the keycard, then its
  door, then the exit), a yellow diamond with the distance floats over it, or
  an arrow at the screen edge points to it. Anything you haven't found stays
  unmarked. Turn it off in Options for classic exploring.
- **Hits feel like hits:** demons flash white and get shoved back by shots
  (heavy ones barely budge), the view shakes on shots, hits and explosions
  (Options → Screen shake), and the game freezes for a moment on every kill.
- **Medals and best times:** finishing a level can earn PAR, KILLS, ITEMS and
  SECRETS medals. Level Select shows your best time and the medals you've
  earned, and the score screen lights up new ones.
- **Tips:** short green hints appear the first time something new happens.
  Turn them off (or back on to see them again) in Options.
- **Dying** tells you what got you and how to beat it. You retry the level
  with the gear you walked in with, not a pistol start.

## Tips

- The **shotgun** is hidden in the first big room of E1M1. Grab it.
- Shoot the **barrels** when demons stand near them.
- Colored doors need the matching **keycard**.
- Some walls are not what they seem. Press `E` on suspicious walls to find
  **secret areas** — the tally screen keeps score.
- Finish levels fast to beat **PAR time**.

## The Monsters

- **Imp** — throws fireballs. Keep moving sideways.
- **Gnasher** — all mouth, charges you down. Backpedal and blast it.
- **Ember Knight** — the big red one on E1M3. Bring shells.
- **Riley** — the final boss. See below.

## Smarter Demons

- **They hear you.** Gunfire carries through open rooms and open doors, and
  wakes every demon it reaches. Closed doors block the sound. Punching is silent.
- **They find you.** A demon that hears you walks around walls to reach you.
- **They fight each other.** If an imp's fireball hits a gnasher, the gnasher
  turns on the imp. Stand so that demons are between you and the imps, then
  step back and watch. Demons of the same kind don't hurt each other.
- **Wounded gnashers run away**, then come back snarling.
- **Imps strafe** sideways instead of walking into your shotgun.

## Riley, the Final Boss

Riley is the family's AI, here as a sparring hologram. She fights the way she
works in Living Quarry: she **watches how you play**, and she chooses her moves
from what she learns.

- She notices your favourite weapon, how close you fight, which way you dodge,
  whether you stand still, and whether you hide.
- She **tells you what she noticed**, and it's always true: she only says
  things her counters actually saw ("YOU ALWAYS DODGE LEFT. I'M AIMING THERE NOW.").
- Watch for her **attack tell**: her visor and chest flash white just before
  she shoots.
- Three phases. At two-thirds health she calls imps. At one-third she speeds up.
  In gold she's shielded, and shots bounce off.
- **She remembers.** Next time she tells you how you fought her last time.
  Each time she beats you she goes a little easier (up to 3 notches), and she
  says so. Beat her, and she practises for the rematch.

Her brain is `js/riley.js`. The engine lists the moves that are legal right
now, and she picks one; she can never invent a move.

## Tech Notes

- Pure HTML + CSS + JavaScript, zero dependencies, zero asset files.
- Real raycasting engine (the Wolfenstein 3D / DOOM technique): textured
  walls with sliding doors, floor and ceiling casting, distance lighting,
  z-buffered billboard sprites — all software-rendered into a 320×200
  buffer, just like 1993.
- Menus, settings and level progress live in `js/menu.js`; the engine
  builds the screens and the menu draws them.
- All pixel art is drawn in code (`js/art.js`), all sound is synthesized
  with WebAudio (`js/sound.js`).
- Levels are ASCII maps — open `js/levels.js` and make your own! Every
  letter is explained at the top of the file. The game checks your map at
  load and tells you if a row is the wrong length.
